---
title: "Connect your Salesforce org to Synapse for analysis"
description: "Link a Production, Sandbox, or DevHub Salesforce org to Synapse using OAuth so the platform can read your metadata and analyze your automations."
---

Synapse connects to your Salesforce org using OAuth, so you never need to share a password. Once connected, Synapse can read your org's metadata — objects, fields, Apex classes, flows, and more — to power its analysis and AI-generated recommendations. You can connect multiple orgs (for example, a Sandbox for development and Production for monitoring) and switch between them at any time.

## Prerequisites

Before you add a connection, make sure you have:

- A Synapse account (sign up at [synapse-architect.com](https://synapse-architect.com) if you haven't yet)
- A Salesforce user account in the org you want to connect with at least **Read** access to metadata
- For DevHub orgs: System Administrator profile or equivalent, since scratch org management requires elevated permissions
- Pop-ups allowed for `synapse-architect.com` in your browser (the OAuth window opens as a pop-up)

## Connect a Salesforce org

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings">
    In the Synapse navigation bar, click your account avatar or the gear icon in the top-right corner, then select **Settings**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Go to Connections">
    In the Settings sidebar, click **Connections**. This page shows all your currently connected services, including any existing Salesforce orgs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a connection">
    Click **Add connection** and choose **Salesforce** from the list of available integrations.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose your org type">
    Select the type of org you are connecting:

    | Org type | When to use |
    |----------|-------------|
    | **Production** | Your live Salesforce org with real customer data |
    | **Sandbox** | A development or testing copy of your Production org |
    | **Developer / DevHub** | A Developer Edition or DevHub org for scratch org management |

    Synapse uses this label to display environment badges throughout the UI and to warn you when analysis affects production metadata.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Authenticate with Salesforce">
    Click **Connect with Salesforce**. A Salesforce login pop-up will open. Sign in with the credentials for the org you want to connect — make sure you're logging in to the correct org type (use the **Use Custom Domain** link if your org has a custom login URL).

    After you log in, Salesforce will ask you to approve the permissions Synapse is requesting. Review them and click **Allow**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm the connection">
    Once authentication succeeds, the pop-up closes and your new org appears in the Connections list with a green **Active** badge. Synapse automatically fetches a metadata overview in the background — this usually takes less than a minute.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Permissions Synapse requests

Synapse requests the following OAuth scopes from Salesforce:

| Scope | What Synapse uses it for |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **Access and manage your data (api)** | Reading Salesforce metadata via the Metadata API and Tooling API |
| **Perform requests at any time (refresh_token)** | Keeping the connection active without requiring you to log in again |
| **Access custom permissions** | Reading permission sets and custom permission metadata |

Synapse does **not** write data to your Salesforce org. All operations are read-only unless you explicitly trigger a deployment through the platform.

<Note>
  Your OAuth tokens are stored encrypted in Synapse's database. Synapse refreshes tokens automatically before they expire. If a token cannot be refreshed — for example, if a Salesforce admin revokes the connected app's access — your connection will show an **Expired** status and you'll need to re-authenticate.
</Note>

## Connect multiple orgs

You can connect as many Salesforce orgs as you need. A common setup is:

- **Production** — monitored for governor limit risk and dependency analysis
- **Sandbox (Full or Partial Copy)** — used for pre-deployment what-if analysis
- **Developer / DevHub** — used for scratch org workflows and CI/CD integration

To add another org, repeat the steps above. Each org appears as a separate entry in your Connections list, labeled with its org type badge. When you work in Synapse — for example, running a metadata sync or a governor limit analysis — you choose which connected org to target.

## Check connection health

On the **Connections** page, each Salesforce org shows a status indicator:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Active** | Token is valid; Synapse can access the org |
| **Expired** | Token expired or was revoked — click **Refresh** to re-authenticate |
| **Error** | An unexpected error occurred — hover for details or try refreshing |

If a connection shows **Expired**, click the refresh icon next to it. This opens the Salesforce OAuth flow again so you can issue a new token without removing and re-adding the connection.

<Warning>
  If a Salesforce administrator revokes the Synapse connected app from your org's **Connected Apps** settings, your connection will stop working immediately. Contact your Salesforce admin to restore access, then click **Refresh** in Synapse to re-authenticate.
</Warning>

## Remove a connection

To disconnect a Salesforce org:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Connections">
    Go to **Settings → Connections**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the org">
    Locate the Salesforce org you want to remove.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delete the connection">
    Click the **···** menu next to the org and select **Remove connection**. Confirm when prompted.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Removing a connection deletes the stored OAuth token and removes the org from all dropdowns in Synapse. It does not affect anything in Salesforce itself — the connected app entry in your org remains unless you also revoke it there.
